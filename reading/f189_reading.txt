Cover names (not resolved; hypotheses only): "nostre oncle" (context: the Pope, who made a promise "to the
man who went to see him"); "l'homme de la chesne" (chain or oak; context: the party that gives only words,
i.e. Spain or its ambassador in Rome); "l'homme du colege" / "nostre ami du colege" (a cardinal);
"nostre docteur Gratian" (context of ll. 6-8 and 28-32: the legate Sega or a man of his legation);
"le monarque" (the King of Spain); "vostre auditeur" (the papal commissary who disbanded the papal troops).

TRANSLATION
Our will was good, and our power great enough to make it succeed; but whether we could have held out in the
long run, that is what made us keep still. Meanwhile our friends showed themselves not only neutral but open
enemies, and did what they could to ruin the friend(?) of the man of the College, and yours, sparing nothing,
putting up now one candidate, now another. Our Doctor Gratian seconded them; he played the part better than
any of the others, so far as to stake his master's authority on it, indeed to compromise it and do it much
harm. If our uncle had been willing to speak as he promised the man who went to see him, and if the man of
the chain had given other assurances than words, we would have gone in head down. But believe me, a man who
is far away could not think of this: he speaks of it as a blind man speaks of colours. The best that could
be done was protection, and to gain time. That was not done; the truce was necessary. The imprudence of the
monarch and his people was such that he had made no preparation, or so small a one that the least gentleman
in a quarrel would have done more. On your side, the disbanding that your auditor carried out was another
fine deed. I want to believe what you write, that nothing has changed; yet it is strange that our Poquinteste
brought us back nothing in particular, and that they told us what they pleased; and it was only words, the
most extravagant I could have imagined. To lose everything, even to be shut out of paradise: I would have
gone much further; to toil, to ruin oneself, to do all that can be done, so that without our prudence we
would not last an hour; and for reward, to build a ruin. I would not be so charitable, I confess. If our
friend of the College would speak of it to the man of the chain, he would greatly oblige our friend, who is
wholly his. As to your pension, it cannot be done, for no one supports it but the prelate to whom the
benefice is assigned, and this is not the time to take anything from him. That was your bad luck and mine.
Courage; other occasions will come. I hold that we shall have the truce in a few days. The journey of [sign]
was the most pernicious thing in the world: it made [the King of Navarre] change his religion, and it offers
[500?] thousand ecus. Their party was ruined without this change. Our legate did what he could to cover the
Spaniard; he took up their cause with such ardour that he neglected his master's service, so far as not to
deliver even one of our briefs, which would have done great good. But that was not what he wanted; on the
contrary, [he wanted] to hasten the dismemberment of our state. From the letter to [His Holiness?] you will
know the true general state of our affairs. I kiss your hands ... Paris, 22 July 1593.
