# Desportes to Pietro Aldobrandini, Paris, 22 July 1593

Paris, BnF, ms. français 3984, f. 186 recto and verso (Gallica: ark btv1b9060633d, images 347 and 348). A letter in
clear French to the Pope's nephew, with long passages in cipher: about 41 cipher lines on the recto and 33 on the
verso. The letter opens "Illustrissimo Monseigneur, Si je nay escrit a Sa Sainteté et a Vostre Seigneurie
Illustrissime aussi souvent que le devoir me le commande ...".

**Status: working draft, version 0.1 (1 October 2026).** Only the cipher passages are given here. The machine reading
is in [raw/read_f186r.txt](raw/read_f186r.txt) and [raw/read_f186v.txt](raw/read_f186v.txt), with the sign strings in
the two `dump_` files. A person has not yet checked it sign by sign. `[clair]` in the raw files marks a stretch of
clear text between two cipher passages. `#` marks a sign for a name or title that is not yet resolved.

## Selected passages

Line numbers are those of the raw files (they start at 0). Spelling is that of the cipher text.

### Recto, lines 18 to 26: the Spanish army, the election of a king, and the Mass on Sunday

> ... larmee du [sign] na rien fait sinon de demeurer sur la frontiere, seruant d'oprobre a un si grand monarque, et
> qui sest retiree seulement sur un bruit que [sign] laloit chercher. Et outre [sign] sans consideration quelconque
> ueult que nous en uenions a lelection dun roy, lors que lennemy commence a gagner les uoix de beaucoup qui luy
> estoient ennemis, par laparance quil fait de se uouloir faire catolique, **et doit dimanche aller a la messe**.
> Nauoir rien pour luy resister sinon des promesses, ou lon a tant este trompe, il faudroit beaucoup [..] auant que
> de nous faire reiecter laparence dune paix, et ne sera peu fait si nous la moderons par une trefue, un chacun
> estant las de seruir de fable a toute la crestiente et de se uoir abandonnez ...

Translation:

> ... the army of [sign] has done nothing but stay on the frontier, a disgrace to so great a monarch, and it
> withdrew on a mere rumour that [sign] was coming to seek it. And besides, [sign], with no consideration at all,
> wants us to proceed to the election of a king, at the moment when the enemy begins to win the voices of many who
> were his enemies, by the show he makes of wishing to become a Catholic, **and on Sunday he is to go to Mass**.
> With nothing to resist him but promises, by which we have been deceived so often, it would take a great deal
> [..] to make us reject the appearance of a peace, and it will be no small thing if we temper it with a truce,
> everyone being tired of serving as a tale for all Christendom and of seeing themselves abandoned ...

The Sunday after 22 July 1593 was 25 July, the day Henri IV abjured at Saint-Denis and heard Mass.

### Recto, lines 28 to 31: the Holy See and its costs

> ... pour en cas quil se face catolique destre receu. Le [sainct] siege, lasse de continuer sa despence, que lon
> peut dire auoir este quelque [chose] durant le pontificat du [sign] Gregoire ...

Translation:

> ... so that, if he becomes a Catholic, he will be received. The Holy See, tired of continuing its spending, which
> one may say was something under the pontificate of Gregory ...

Gregory XIV (1590 to 1591) paid for an army in support of the League.

### Verso, lines 17 to 23: the patience of the writer's master

> si elle sauoit comme moy lartifice de nos amis ... elle sestonneroit mil et mil fois de la patience de [sign], qui
> sest port[e] iusques a uouloir accepter [sign] pour roy, pourueu que lon y feist uoir le moien de le pouuoir
> deffendre. Mais on ueut que lon [se paie] en paroles ... des gens qui ne nous firent iamais paroistre en auoir, et
> qui ont pour maxime de nous uouloir tromper.

Translation:

> If you knew, as I do, the artifice of our friends ... you would marvel a thousand times at the patience of
> [sign], who went so far as to be willing to accept [sign] as king, provided he was shown the means to defend him.
> But they want us to be paid in words ... people who never showed us that they had any, and whose maxim is to
> deceive us.

### Verso, lines 28 to 32: a quarrel

> ... et puis les choses en sont entre eulx en telz termes que sans le respect de [sign] il ne se ioueroit plus si
> lon[g] temps a ses despens ny de la ruine de tous ceulx de sa maison ... un chacun pense estre artifice pour
> paruenir a ses desseins, affin dobliger lespagnol, qui a este tousiours sa principalle fin.

Translation:

> ... and then things between them stand so that, but for the respect owed to [sign], he would no longer play so
> long at his expense, nor at the ruin of all those of his house ... everyone thinks it a trick to reach his own
> ends, so as to oblige the Spaniard, which has always been his chief aim.
