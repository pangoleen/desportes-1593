# Prior-art check: printed scholarship and editions, 1600 to today

Subject: BnF, ms. français 3984, f. 189 (Desportes to Girolamo Frachetta, Paris, 22 July 1593, wholly in cipher) and the cipher passages of f. 186r-v (Desportes to Pietro Aldobrandini, same date).

Date of this check: 1 October 2026. Method: web research only. I did not go to a library.

Each entry keeps two things apart: what the source states, and what I infer.

---

## 1. Verdict in short

**No earlier printed text, calendar, summary or decipherment of f. 189 or of the cipher passages of f. 186 was found in these sources.**

Three facts make this verdict stronger than a plain "nothing found":

1. The royalist office that deciphered the sibling letters in 1593 wrote that it did **not** decipher these two. This statement is in BnF fr. 3983, f. 141v, and again on the back of f. 189 itself (section 3).
2. The BnF catalogue (printed 1868-1881, now online) gives "avec chiffre et déchiffrement" for the sibling letters, but only "avec chiffre" for nos. 88 and 90. No other item of fr. 3974-3995 is a decipherment of them (section 2).
3. Every scholar who used these leaves (L'Épinois 1886, Richard 1901, Lavaud 1936, Drouot 1937, Baldini 1981-1997) quotes only the letters that were deciphered in 1593, or does not use the July letters at all (section 4).

One real gap stays open. Frachetta gave his letters from Mayenne and Desportes to the Spanish ambassador in Rome in September 1593. Copies are at Simancas (Estado, leg. 961 and 963). Baldini describes them, and his description does not include a letter of 22 July 1593. Nobody can be sure until someone looks at those two bundles (section 5).

## 2. BnF catalogue notice of fr. 3974-3995

Source: BnF, Archives et manuscrits, "Français 3974-3995, Collection Mémoires de la Ligue", https://archivesetmanuscrits.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/cc504266 . The same text is in the Gallica record of fr. 3984, https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b9060633d , and in the printed *Catalogue des manuscrits français. Ancien fonds*, vol. 3 (Paris, 1881).

What the notice states (verbatim, fr. 3984):

- Fol. 176, no. 84: Lettre, avec chiffre et déchiffrement, de « BAUDOUYN DESPORTES » au pape Clément VIII. « De Paris, ce XXIIe juillet 1593 ».
- Fol. 184, no. 87: « Deschiffrement de la lectre de BAUDOUYN DESPORTES à Mr [l'évêque] de Lizieux... De Paris, ce XXIIe juillet 1593 ». Fol. 185: à la suite un postscriptum du 26 juillet 1593.
- Fol. 186, no. 88: Lettre, avec chiffre, de BAUDOUIN « DESPORTES,... à monsgr don Pietro Aldobrandini,... De Paris, ce XXIIe juillet 1593 ».
- Fol. 188, no. 89: Original de la lettre déchiffrée sous le n° 87.
- Fol. 189, no. 90: Lettre, avec chiffre, de BAUDOUIN DESPORTES à « Hieronimo Frachetta,... De Paris, ce XXIIe juillet 1593 ».
- Fol. 274, no. 115: Lettre, avec chiffre et déchiffrement, d'« ANNE DE GIVRY, e[vêque] de Lisieux... à Mr Desportes, Sr de Beuvillier,... A Rome, juillet 1593 ».

Other items in the 22 volumes that name Desportes (I searched the full notice for "Desportes", "Frachett", "Beuvill" and "intercept"):

- fr. 3982, no. 51: « Extraict d'une lettre escripte par DESPORTES estant à Romme, au Sr Roussieux, du 17 novembre 1592 ». No. 65 (f. 149): the letter itself, to "monseigneur de Royssieu", Rome, 17 November 1592.
- fr. 3983, no. 7 (f. 19): « Advis de Rome » on the « negociation de Mr de Lizieulx et Desportes » with Clement VIII, 13 January 1593.
- fr. 3983, no. 72 (ff. 140-144): « Extraict de lectres interceptés, tant en chiffre qu'aultrement ». The notice lists entries a to z. Entries j, k, l are: Lisieux to "Desportes, Sr de Beuvilliers", July 1593; Desportes to the Pope, 22 July 1593; Desportes to Lisieux, 22 and 26 July 1593.
- fr. 3994, no. 164 (f. 213): Desportes to the duke of Nevers, Paris, 6 October 1595.

"Frachetta" occurs once in the whole notice (no. 90).

What I infer:

- No item of fr. 3984, or of fr. 3974-3983 and 3985-3995, is described as the decipherment of no. 88 or no. 90.
- In the catalogue's own convention, text inside « » is copied from the document. The name "Baudouyn" is inside the marks for nos. 84 and 87 only. So "Baudouyn Desportes" is the wording of the 1593 clerk on the decipherments. For nos. 88 and 90 the cataloguer supplied "Baudouin".
- The notice of fr. 3983 no. 72 is not complete. It stops at entry l and leaves out two short entries that follow it on the same page (section 3).

## 3. Contemporary statement that the two letters were not deciphered (manuscript, seen on Gallica)

This is a manuscript, not printed scholarship. I looked at it because the catalogue pointed to it. Please check my reading on the full image.

**BnF fr. 3983, f. 141r-v**, part of no. 72, « Extraict de lectres interceptés ». Gallica: https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b9059406b/f249.item (f. 141r) and https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b9059406b/f250.item (f. 141v).

What the source states (my transcription):

- f. 141r: « Par la l[ett]re de M. l'evesque de Lizieux à Desportes sr de Beuvilliers du mois de juillet 1593, cottée 10 ».
- f. 141r: « Par celle dud. Desportes à Sa Saincteté en datte du 22 juillet aud. an, cottée avec son deschiffrem[en]t 11 ». The extract begins: « Qu'il a plusieurs fois asseuré Sa S[ainte]té qu'en cas que le R. d'Espaigne persistast à vouloir sa fille royne ou le duc Erneste ou aultre contre nos loix, il seroit occasion de la ruyne du party et de l'establissement du R. de N. »
- f. 141v: « Par l[ett]re dud. Desportes à l'evesque de Lizieux des 22 et 26 juillet aussi en chiffre, avecq son deschiffrement, cottée 12 ».
- f. 141v: « 13. C'est une semblable l[ett]re en chiffre dud. Desportes au sieur Pietre Aldobrandin, nepveu de Sa Saincteté, **que l'on n'a eu loisir encores de deschiffrer**, mais doibt estre de mesme subgect, et est du mesme datte 22 juillet 1593. »
- f. 141v: « 14. C'est encore une pareille lettre en mesme chiffre d'icelluy Desportes au seigneur Hieronime Frachette, **qui n'a non plus encore esté deschiffrée**, et est du mesme datte et de mesme chiffre que les trois précédentes. »

**BnF fr. 3984, f. 189v** (Gallica view 354, https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/btv1b9060633d/f354.item ). The address reads « Au Seigneur Hieronimo Frachetta ». A royalist endorsement along the edge ends: « ... encore deschiffrée. Est à notter que c'est le mesme chiffre que celle de Sa Saincteté et de Mr de Lizieux. » A large « 14 » is written below it. The first words of the endorsement are in the gutter of my crop; read them on the full image.

**BnF fr. 3984, f. 186v** (Gallica view 348). The letter to Aldobrandini ends « tres humble et tres obeissant serviteur / Desportes ». The signature is "Desportes" alone.

What I infer:

- The numbers 10 to 14 are the royalist office's file numbers ("cottée"). The "14" on f. 189v ties that leaf to entry 14 of the extract.
- When the extract was made, the office had deciphered the letters to the Pope and to Lisieux and had left the letters to Aldobrandini and Frachetta in cipher. The extract includes letters up to 22 August 1593, so it dates from late August or September 1593.
- No later hand added a decipherment to either place. Together with the catalogue, this is good evidence that no decipherment was made in 1593.

## 5. Italian and Spanish side

What sources state:

- **Vatican.** L'Épinois cites received letters of Desportes to the Pope of 6 and 8 April 1593 (his "t. XLIII, f° 244, 246"). Richard cites an Italian translation of a Desportes dispatch in "Nunz., tome 34, f°s 439-443". Drouot points to Nunziatura di Francia 37 and Fondo Borghese I 232 for Sega's letters of 1592-1593. So the Vatican keeps Desportes letters that did arrive. None of these authors cites a Vatican copy of a letter of 22 July 1593.
- **Simancas.** Estado 961 and 963 hold ciphered copies of Frachetta's correspondence with Mayenne and Desportes, sent by the duke of Sessa from 20 September 1593 to 21 February 1594 (Baldini). The letters from Desportes that Baldini describes are dated October 1593 to February 1594.
- **Modena.** Archivio di Stato, Ambasciatori Roma 152-156: two letters of Desportes that Frachetta passed to the Ferrarese agent from December 1593 (Baldini).
- **No edition.** I found no printed edition of Frachetta's letters, of Desportes's letters, or of Sega's legation. A.-C. Tizon-Germe's École des chartes thesis (1990) and her article in *Bibliothèque de l'École des chartes* 151 (1993), https://www.persee.fr/doc/bec_0373-6237_1993_num_151_1_450683 , study the legation and do not edit the letters. I saw no *Acta Nuntiaturae Gallicae* volume for Sega; I did not verify the series list.

What I infer:

- The four letters of 22 July 1593 in Paris are the sealed originals with their addresses. So these copies did not reach Rome. Sega's own complaint and Nevers's answer (section 4.1) say that packets were taken at Nevers.
- A duplicate by another road is possible. If Frachetta received one, it would be among the papers he gave to Sessa in September 1593. Baldini's description has no letter of July 1593, so this is not likely, but I cannot exclude it.
- The Aldobrandini family archive (Frascati; part with Doria Pamphilj, Rome) could hold a duplicate of the letter to Pietro Aldobrandini. I found no inventory or study that says so.

## 6. Who is "Baudouin-Desportes"?

What sources state:

- 1593 royalist clerk: the decipherments are headed for a letter of « Baudouyn Desportes » (BnF notice, nos. 84 and 87). The extract in fr. 3983 f. 141 names the addressee of Lisieux's letter « Desportes sr de Beuvilliers » and then calls the writer of the four letters of 22 July « ledit Desportes ».
- f. 186v is signed « Desportes ».
- Contemporary and near-contemporary print: « Desportes nommé Baudoüin, Secretaire de Monsieur de Mayenne » (tract in *Mémoires de Nevers*); « Desportes Baudouin », one of Mayenne's four secretaries of state (Palma Cayet; Perroniana); « Bauldouin-Desportes », secretary of state, who read Mayenne's declaration to the Estates on 26 January 1593 (Bernard).
- 19th century (Bernard, Abord, L'Épinois, Richard): Baudouin, Philippe and Jacques Desportes were brothers. The BnF index has one heading "Desportes, Baudouin, sieur de Bevilliers" and another "Desportes, Thibaut, sieur de Bevilliers".
- Lavaud (1936) and Drouot (1937) correct this. They separate two men. **Thibault Desportes**, the poet's younger brother, later sieur de Bévilliers and grand audiencier de France, was a secretary of Mayenne and his envoy to Rome in 1590-91 and 1592-93. **Baudouin Desportes** ("Desportes-Baudouin") was Mayenne's secretary of state, signed acts "Baudouin", and was a cousin of Thibault; Lavaud could not find the degree.
- Baldini, from the Simancas papers, names Thibault as Frachetta's patron and correspondent, and says Frachetta dealt with Pietro Aldobrandini for him.
- Thibault's later titles are certain: a notarial receipt names him « conseiller du Roi et grand audiencier de la Chancellerie de France, sieur de Beuvilliers », heir of his brother Philippe, abbot of Tiron ( https://francearchives.fr/facomponent/b4c7252436d2162745867d20fb910bd50b6ea67a ).

What I infer:

- The writer of f. 186 and f. 189 is **most probably Thibault Desportes**, brother of the poet. Reasons: the letters go to the same Roman circle he had just left (the Pope, Pietro Aldobrandini, the bishop of Lisieux, his agent Frachetta); Lisieux's reply in the same cipher is addressed to "Desportes, Sr de Beuvillier"; the royalist extract treats that man and the writer as one; f. 189 speaks of Frachetta's pension, which fits the patron.
- The name "Baudouyn" on the decipherments is then the clerk's mistake, or the clerk believed that the two men were one. The BnF cataloguers made the same merge.
- I cannot exclude that the writer is the secretary of state Baudouin. The clerk's heading says so, and Sega's remark about "the secretary Desportes" could mean either man.
- So the claim "the writer is of the family of the poet" is safe in both cases. The claim "brother of the poet" is probable, not proved.

Two loose ends:

- Local historians say Thibault bought Bévilliers (Choisel) in 1596. The title "Sr de Beuvillier" is already on the letter of July 1593, and Breunot calls him "Beauvilliers" in 1595. Lavaud (pp. 324, 436-437) should explain this.
- Breunot once calls this man « neveu de M. de Tiron ». Lavaud reads it as the brother.

How to settle it: compare the hand of ff. 186 and 188 with the autograph letter from Rome of 17 November 1592 (fr. 3982, f. 149) and the letter to Nevers of 6 October 1595 (fr. 3994, f. 213), and with a "Baudouin" countersignature on an act of Mayenne. Read Lavaud pp. 362-366 in full.

## 7. History of the letters (short)

Stated by sources: the letters are dated Paris, 22 July 1593 (the one to Lisieux has a postscript of 26 July). In August 1593 Sega told Rome that Nevers had taken at Nevers the letters that did not arrive. Nevers did not deny that packets were taken there. The royalist office gave the letters file numbers 10 to 14, deciphered three, and noted that it had no time for the other two. The extract with those notes is in Nevers's own papers.

Inferred: the packet left Paris on or after 26 July, was taken on the road in Nevers's government in late July or August 1593, and was worked on before Nevers left for Rome in the autumn. The extract was probably made for that embassy. The two letters stayed in cipher from 1593 to 2026.

## 9. Verdict

**None found in these sources.** No printed text, calendar entry, summary or decipherment of BnF fr. 3984 f. 189, or of the cipher passages of f. 186, exists in the works and databases listed above.

The evidence goes further than silence. The 1593 royalist office recorded that it left both letters in cipher (fr. 3983 f. 141v, entries 13 and 14; endorsement on fr. 3984 f. 189v). The catalogue, Tomokiyo (2017-2023) and every historian who used the volume agree with that.

The claim of a first reading stands, with one condition: Simancas, Estado 961 and 963, are not yet checked for a duplicate of the letter to Frachetta.
