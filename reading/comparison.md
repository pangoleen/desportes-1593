# What is new in the two letters? A comparison of the four letters of 22 July 1593

Made on 3 October 2026, in answer to S. Tomokiyo's question
(https://cryptiana.web.fc2.com/code/polyphonic1593.htm): do the letters to Aldobrandini (f. 186) and to
Frachetta (f. 189) add something to the letters that the royal office deciphered in 1593?

**Status: first comparison.** It was made by a model from four texts. The transcription of f. 184 was read twice
by the model; no palaeographer has checked it. A historian must confirm each point marked "new" against the literature.

## The four texts

| Office no. | Letter | Cipher | Text used here | Read in 1593? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | To Pope Clement VIII | f. 176r-v, 179r | Office decipherment f. 177r-v (`gold/f177_f178_office_decipherment_v2.txt`); f. 179r from our gold lines | Yes |
| 12 | To the bishop of Lisieux, 22 July, with a postscript of 26 July | f. 188r-v | Office decipherment f. 184r-v (`gold/f184_office_decipherment_1593.txt`, new on 3 Oct 2026) and f. 185r | Yes |
| 13 | To Pietro Aldobrandini | f. 186r-v | Our reading (`reading/f186_reading.txt`) | No |
| 14 | To Girolamo Frachetta | f. 189 | Our reading (`reading/f189_reading.txt`) | No |

## Short answer

1. **The office guessed right.** It wrote of letter 13 that it "doibt estre de mesme subgect". The main
   argument of the letter to Aldobrandini is the argument of the letters to the Pope and to Lisieux: the legate
   serves Spain, Spain gives only words, the League needs a truce, and Navarre's conversion wins the country.
2. **The forecast of the conversion on Sunday is not new.** The office read it in letter 12. Desportes writes to
   Lisieux: "Pour le R. de N. Il se doibt faire cath. dimanche" (f. 184r, l. 31). The letter to Aldobrandini says
   the same thing with the words "et doit dimanche aller a la messe". The letter to Lisieux gives more detail
   (see below).
3. **Seven passages are new.** They are in no letter that the office read. The list follows.
4. **Three passages occur in a read letter and in an unread letter.** They agree in fact and differ in words.
   This is an independent check of our reading.

## A. Content found only in the two unread letters

### Letter to Aldobrandini (f. 186)

| Lines | Content | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| r11-17 | Attack by name on the papal commissaries Matteucci and Malvasia: "eulx seulx ont plus aporté de ruine aux affaires que les ennemis". Desportes gave the Pope a "discours" on Matteucci's conduct. Malvasia and Mayenne fell out; the legate's nephew complained. | The letters to the Pope and to Lisieux name neither man. A separate written "discours" against Matteucci existed. |
| r17-20 | The army of the King of Spain "n'a rien fait sinon de demeurer sur la frontiere" and withdrew "seulement sur un bruit" that Navarre came to seek it. | The letter to the Pope says only that the Spanish ministers lack forces. |
| r28-32 | The Holy See is tired of its expense, which was "quelque chose" under Pope Gregory [XIV]; it would have been better to refuse all help at once. | A reproach to Rome that Desportes does not make to the Pope himself. |
| r32-39 | Desportes recalls his own negotiation in Rome: he asked for "un de la maison de Bourbon" and was refused; then for the marriage of the Infanta "avec un de nos princes", and was refused and sent to the legate and to the Duke of Feria. | The letter to the Pope only alludes to "le temps que j'euz cest honneur de traicter de ceste matiere". Here are the two requests and the two refusals. |
| r39-42 | He rejected "ce sujet qui me fust proposé de [Sa Sainteté], que a la verité eust esté le meilleur". | The Pope proposed a candidate of his own, and Mayenne's envoy refused him. The letter does not name the candidate. The next words ("ores que mon Maistre s'i fust voulu laisser aller ... Et ou en seroit il maintenant?") suggest that the proposal concerned Mayenne himself or his house. This is an inference. |
| v0-15 | Defence of Mayenne. The ministers of the King of Spain "ne seroient pas hors de ranc ny de trop bonne maison pour tenir lieu de serviteurs". Good "apologies" will be written. The one who shows only religion has "pure ambition trop descouverte". | Tone and personal attack that the other letters do not have. |
| v19-21 | Mayenne "s'est porté jusques a vouloir accepter Monsieur [de Guise] pour roy, pourveu que l'on y feist voir le moien de le pouvoir deffendre". | The letter to the Pope says that the Spanish offer of Guise and the Infanta is a trick. Only this letter says that Mayenne was ready to accept Guise as king, on a condition. |

### Letter to Frachetta (f. 189)

| Lines | Content | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| whole | A private channel in cover names: "nostre oncle", "l'homme de la chesne", "l'homme du colege", "nostre docteur Gratian", "le monarque", "vostre auditeur". | None of the other three letters uses cover names. The names are not resolved. |
| 8-10 | "Si nostre oncle eust voulu parler suivant la promesse qu'il en avoit faite a celuy qui l'ala trouver ... la teste baissee on y eust entré." | A promise made in Rome and not kept. If "nostre oncle" is the Pope, this is a direct charge. |
| 14-15 | "le licentiement que feist vostre auditeur": the disbanding (of the papal troops) is "un autre bel acte". | Not in the read letters. |
| 23-25 | Frachetta's pension "ne se peult": only the prelate who holds the benefice supports it. | Personal business of the agent. It fits what Baldini says of Frachetta (see `prior_art/scholarship.md`). |
| 26-28 | "Le voiage de [sign] a esté le plus pernicieulx que chose du monde: il a fait changer de religion au [roy de Navarre] et offre [500?] mil escus. Leur party estoit ruiné sans se changement." | A named journey is blamed for the conversion, with a sum of money. The sign for the traveller is not in any other letter and is not read. |
| 29-32 | The legate neglected his master's service "jusques a n'avoir fait tenir un seul de nos brefz", so as "de haster le desmembrement de nostre estat". | A precise charge: the legate held back papal briefs. The read letters do not make it. The reading "brefz" carries a (?). |

## C. Content that only the READ letters have

The office knew these points in 1593. The two unread letters do not repeat them.

- **Who prepares the conversion** (Lisieux, f. 184r l. 31-34): "Le Perron l'instruict"; the curé of
  Saint-Eustache "y a esté convié", with Chavignac, Morenne, the bishop of Bayeux and others; all the presidents
  of Navarre's parlements and the nobility are to attend. Desportes himself "traicté avec ledict Perron plus de
  sept heures". His conclusion: "nous pensions faire un coup d'estat par noz estatz & qu'il nous le rendent
  inutille".
- **Military state** (Pope, f. 177r-v): the fall of Dreux; within two weeks Navarre has 3,000 horse and
  12,000 foot ("xij m", a roman figure in clear); Mayenne and the princes have fewer than 200 horse.
  (Our first transcription of f. 177 had "vingt mil hommes" and "dix pieces" here. Both were
  misreadings of ours; see `evidence/decipherer_1593.md`.)
- **The legate's motive** (Pope, margin of f. 177v): "c'est de le faire pape".
- **Mayenne's poverty** (Lisieux, f. 184r l. 37-38): "Il y a deux mois qu'il ne vit que d'emprunt".
- **The abjuration itself** (Lisieux, postscript of 26 July, f. 185r): the candle, the church door refused, the
  high Mass by the archbishop of Bourges, vespers, Montmartre, the bonfires.

## D. Three cross-checks of our reading

Each of these facts is in a letter that the office deciphered in 1593 and in a letter that we read in 2026.
The words differ, so one text is not a copy of the other.

1. Sunday: "se doibt faire cath. dimanche" (1593) and "doit dimanche aller a la messe" (2026).
2. The Pope's letter with the three points: "ne rien faire au prejudice de Bourbon ... alterer les catholicques
   qui sont avec le R. de N." (1593) and "ne rien faire au prejudice de la maison de Bourbon ... ny contre les
   catoliques qui assistent [le roy de Navarre]" (2026).
3. The name in f. 189 l. 17. We could not read it and printed the sign classes "C B + D H I A G E F G E =
   co quinteste??". In the letter to Lisieux, Desportes wrote the same name in CLEAR text, in his own hand:
   "de poquinteste sur ce que l'on ne nous aprenoit mille nouvelles" (f. 188r, l. 10; checked on the native
   image). The office clerk copied it on f. 184r. Both contexts are the same: a man came back and brought
   nothing. So the cipher word of f. 189 is "po" + "quinteste". The person is not identified; a web search
   found nothing. Note: the first ten lines of f. 188r are mostly clear text with cipher words mixed in.

## E. Open points

- The transcription of f. 184 needs a check by a palaeographer. Four doubtful words carry (?).
- The person "Poquinteste".
- The candidate "proposé de Sa Sainteté" (f. 186 r39-40).
- The sign after "le voiage de" (f. 189 l. 26) and the sum.
- Whether historians have used the office decipherments of letters 11 and 12. If they have not, Section C is
  also little known.
